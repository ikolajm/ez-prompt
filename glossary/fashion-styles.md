# Fashion Styles

Fashion movements, eras, and subcultures — each with a distinct visual vocabulary of silhouette, fabric, color, and cultural signaling. In prompt composition, a fashion style descriptor controls not just what the subject wears but the entire mood, era, and cultural context of the image.

---

### Haute Couture

**What it is**: Custom-fitted, handmade high fashion from Paris couture houses. Extreme attention to detail — hand-sewn, hand-finished, using luxury fabrics (silk dupioni, brocade, cashmere, organza). Each piece is one-of-a-kind, fitted to a specific client. The pinnacle of fashion as art.

**Visual effect**: Exquisite, sculptural, otherworldly. Garments have visible construction quality — fabric drapes perfectly, seams are invisible, details are impossibly fine. Silhouettes range from architectural to flowing, but always extreme and intentional. Reads as luxury, exclusivity, and fashion-as-art.

**Pairs well with**:
- Studio/controlled lighting — the craftsmanship needs to be visible
- Clean/elegant backgrounds — the garment is the focus
- Medium format / high-resolution — detail matters
- Dramatic/editorial poses — the clothing commands attention
- Rich/jewel-toned or pristine white fabric — luxury materials
- Beauty lighting (butterfly, clamshell) — flattering the garment and wearer

**Conflicts with**:
- Casual/everyday context — haute couture is extraordinary by definition
- Lo-fi/snapshot aesthetic — the quality demands precision
- Streetwear energy — different fashion vocabulary entirely
- Gritty/raw environments — the garments are too precious

**Example combination**: Haute couture + studio lighting + clean background + medium format + dramatic pose → sculptural garment catching light, fabric quality visible, construction perfection, fashion editorial at the highest level

### Streetwear

**What it is**: Casual fashion rooted in hip-hop, skateboarding, surf culture, and sneaker culture. Oversized fits, graphic tees, hoodies, sneakers (Air Jordans, Dunks, Air Force 1s), workwear jackets, and bold logo branding. Brands: Supreme, Stüssy, BAPE, Palace, Off-White. New York hip-hop + California skate = streetwear.

**Visual effect**: Casual, logo-forward, layered. Oversized proportions — hoodies, baggy pants, chunky sneakers. Bold graphics and brand logos are focal points. Sports team apparel mixed with skate/surf brands. The fit is relaxed and intentional about looking unstudied. Reads as youth culture, urban, authentic, and brand-aware.

**Pairs well with**:
- Urban environments — streets, skate parks, storefronts
- Direct flash / snapshot aesthetic — candid, raw energy
- Bold graphics and logos — branding is the point
- Sneakers as focal point — the shoe is often the hero
- Oversized proportions — baggy, layered, relaxed
- Bright/saturated color accents — bold colorways on shoes and accessories

**Conflicts with**:
- Formal/structured tailoring — the opposite silhouette
- Haute couture elegance — different universe
- Muted/minimal branding — streetwear wants to be seen
- Studio/controlled settings — streetwear lives in the street

**Example combination**: Streetwear + urban alley + direct flash + oversized hoodie + fresh sneakers → raw street energy, logo visible, sneakers catching flash, casual but deliberate, youth culture

### Techwear

**What it is**: Fashion built from technical/performance fabrics — Gore-Tex, ripstop nylon, Schoeller textiles. Utilitarian design with military and outdoor influence — multiple pockets, straps, buckles, modular attachment points. Dark/monochrome palette (black, charcoal, dark olive). Brands: Acronym, Nike ACG, Arc'teryx Veilance, Stone Island Shadow Project.

**Visual effect**: Dark, functional, futuristic. All-black or near-black outfits with complex construction — articulated joints, magnetic closures, hidden pockets, water-resistant fabric with matte or technical sheen. The silhouette is slim-to-moderate with asymmetric details. Reads as cyberpunk, urban ninja, and dystopian-practical.

**Pairs well with**:
- Dark/monochrome palette — black, charcoal, dark gray
- Urban environments — concrete, glass, rain
- Cyberpunk/near-future aesthetic — the fashion of dystopian cities
- Neon accent lighting — dark clothing lit by neon
- Rain/wet conditions — the waterproof fabric makes sense in rain
- Low-angle shots — the silhouette looks imposing from below

**Conflicts with**:
- Bright/warm color palettes — techwear is dark
- Natural/pastoral environments — too urban and synthetic
- Vintage/retro aesthetics — techwear is forward-looking
- Flowing/draped fabrics — techwear is structured and technical

**Example combination**: Techwear + rainy urban night + neon reflections + all-black outfit + low angle → dark figure in technical clothing, rain beading on fabric, neon reflected in wet surfaces, cyberpunk operative

### Minimalist Fashion

**What it is**: Reduction to essentials — clean lines, neutral colors, quality fabrics, no logos, no decoration. Simple silhouettes executed in premium materials. The fashion of "less is more." Key designers: Jil Sander, COS, The Row, Lemaire, Celine (Phoebe Philo era).

**Visual effect**: Clean, quiet, expensive-looking. Neutral palette — black, white, camel, gray, navy, cream. Simple cuts that rely on proportion and fabric quality rather than embellishment. No visible branding. The luxury is in the material and the fit, not in decoration. Reads as sophisticated, understated, and confidently refined.

**Pairs well with**:
- Neutral/muted palette — the foundation of the style
- Clean backgrounds — the simplicity extends to the environment
- Soft/natural lighting — gentle, not dramatic
- Quality fabric texture — the fabric is the point (cashmere, wool, silk)
- Simple/centered composition — the visual calm extends to framing
- Negative space — minimal fashion + negative space = maximum calm

**Conflicts with**:
- Bold patterns or graphics — too busy
- Bright/saturated color — too loud
- Layered complexity — too many elements
- Logo/brand visibility — minimalism hides its labels

**Example combination**: Minimalist fashion + neutral palette + clean white space + soft natural light + simple composition → quietly luxurious, fabric quality visible, no distraction, sophisticated restraint

### Bohemian / Boho

**What it is**: Free-spirited fashion drawing from 1960s-70s counterculture, folk traditions, and global textiles. Flowing fabrics, layered textures, earth tones mixed with jewel colors. Fringe, embroidery, crochet, wrap skirts, oversized jewelry, headbands, suede boots.

**Visual effect**: Layered, textured, organic. Multiple fabrics and patterns coexist — floral prints next to geometric embroidery. The silhouette is loose and flowing. Natural materials (suede, leather, cotton, linen, wool) dominate. Reads as free-spirited, artistic, well-traveled, and romantic.

**Pairs well with**:
- Warm/golden light — golden hour is the boho lighting
- Natural environments — meadows, deserts, markets, festivals
- Earth tones + jewel accents — brown, rust, gold, turquoise, wine
- Layered textures — multiple fabrics and patterns
- Film aesthetics (Portra, overexposed) — the warm softness matches
- Flowing movement — fabric catching wind

**Conflicts with**:
- Structured/tailored clothing — boho is loose and unstructured
- Urban/industrial settings — too natural and organic
- Monochrome/minimal — boho layers and mixes
- Clean/sharp precision — boho is deliberately relaxed

**Example combination**: Bohemian + golden hour + flowing dress + desert landscape + warm film tones → fabric catching sunset light, layered jewelry, earth and jewel tones, free-spirited and romantic

### Punk

**What it is**: Anti-establishment fashion from 1970s London and New York — leather jackets, ripped clothing, safety pins, studs, band patches, combat boots, mohawks, DIY customization. The visual language of rebellion. Vivienne Westwood and Malcolm McLaren's SEX shop. Ramones, Sex Pistols.

**Visual effect**: Aggressive, DIY, confrontational. Black leather and denim dominant. Deliberately destroyed — ripped, pinned, patched. Metal hardware — studs, chains, zippers, safety pins. Band logos and political statements hand-painted or patched on. The clothing is a statement of refusal. Reads as anti-authority, raw, and self-constructed.

**Pairs well with**:
- Black leather + denim — the uniform
- Direct flash / harsh light — raw, unflattering, honest
- Urban/gritty environments — alleys, clubs, underpasses
- High contrast black and white — the graphic intensity matches
- DIY texture — visible patches, pins, hand-drawn elements
- Confrontational poses — the attitude matches the clothing

**Conflicts with**:
- Polished/luxury aesthetics — punk rejects luxury
- Soft/romantic mood — too aggressive
- Corporate/clean environments — punk is anti-establishment
- Pastel/gentle palettes — punk is black, black, and more black

**Example combination**: Punk + leather jacket + direct flash + urban alley + high contrast B&W → raw, confrontational, studs catching flash, safety pins visible, DIY energy

### Vintage / Retro (Era-Specific)

Fashion referencing specific past decades. Each era has a distinct silhouette and palette:

#### 1920s (Jazz Age / Flapper)

**Visual effect**: Dropped waistlines, beaded fringe, headbands, T-strap shoes. Art Deco geometry in textiles. Straight, boyish silhouette. Luxurious fabrics — silk, velvet, beading.

**Pairs well with**: Art Deco settings, warm amber/gold lighting, sepia or warm grading, geometric patterns.

#### 1950s (Post-War)

**Visual effect**: Cinched waists, full skirts (New Look), Peter Pan collars, saddle shoes, pearls. Structured, feminine, polished. Pastels and bold solids. The silhouette is hourglass.

**Pairs well with**: Bright/clean lighting, Kodachrome-warm color, suburban settings, polished composition.

#### 1960s (Mod / Space Age)

**Visual effect**: Mini skirts, geometric patterns, go-go boots, bold graphic prints. Sharp, youthful, futuristic. Color blocking. The silhouette is straight and short.

**Pairs well with**: Bold graphic patterns, pop art color, clean backgrounds, Twiggy-esque poses.

#### 1970s (Disco / Bohemian)

**Visual effect**: Two tracks — disco (sequins, platform shoes, polyester, halter tops, metallic) and bohemian (flowing, earthy, fringe, denim). Both favor flared/wide-leg silhouettes.

**Pairs well with**: Warm film tones, disco — neon/nightclub lighting; boho — golden hour, natural settings.

#### 1980s (Power / Excess)

**Visual effect**: Power shoulders, neon colors, oversized blazers, leg warmers, bold jewelry, high-waisted everything. Silhouette is broad-shouldered and exaggerated. The fashion of excess and confidence.

**Pairs well with**: Memphis Design colors, harsh/direct lighting, urban settings, bold composition.

#### 1990s (Grunge / Minimal)

**Visual effect**: Two tracks — grunge (flannel, ripped jeans, Doc Martens, oversized band tees, unwashed hair) and minimal (slip dresses, simple silhouettes, muted colors, Kate Moss). Both reject 80s excess.

**Pairs well with**: Grunge — disposable camera, raw flash, Seattle; minimal — clean/soft lighting, muted palette.

### Avant-Garde / Deconstructed

**What it is**: Experimental fashion that challenges conventional clothing structure — asymmetric cuts, raw seams, exposed construction, unconventional materials, garments that question what "clothing" means. Key designers: Comme des Garçons (Rei Kawakubo), Maison Margiela, Rick Owens, Yohji Yamamoto.

**Visual effect**: Challenging, sculptural, intellectual. Garments may be asymmetric, oversized to the point of abstraction, or structurally impossible-looking. Raw edges, visible seams, unfinished details are intentional. Color is often absent (black, white, gray) to focus on form. Reads as high-concept, artistic, and gallery-level fashion.

**Pairs well with**:
- Monochrome/black palette — the standard avant-garde uniform
- Dramatic/theatrical lighting — the sculptural forms need dimension
- Clean/stark backgrounds — the garment shape is the focus
- Unusual poses — the clothing may dictate how the body moves
- Wide or unusual framing — the silhouette may be extreme
- High contrast — emphasizes the sculptural drama

**Conflicts with**:
- Conventional beauty standards — avant-garde challenges them
- Wearable/relatable fashion — the clothes may not be "wearable"
- Bright/cheerful palettes — too playful for the seriousness
- Natural/candid settings — the fashion needs a stage

**Example combination**: Avant-garde + all black + dramatic side light + stark white background + architectural garment → sculptural silhouette, raw seams visible, shadow defining impossible form, gallery-level fashion image

---

### Gorpcore / Outdoor

**What it is**: Outdoor/hiking clothing worn as fashion — fleece jackets, trail shoes, waterproof shells, cargo pants, technical fabrics in nature-inspired colors. Brands: Patagonia, The North Face, Salomon, Arc'teryx. The intersection of performance gear and street fashion.

**Pairs well with**: Natural environments, overcast/soft light, earth tones, functional accessories (carabiners, water bottles).

---

### Old Money / Quiet Luxury

**What it is**: The fashion of understated wealth — cashmere, wool, cotton in neutral tones. No visible logos, no trendy elements. Blazers, loafers, silk scarves, tailored trousers, cable-knit sweaters. The aesthetic of inherited wealth that doesn't need to announce itself. Loro Piana, Brunello Cucinelli, Ralph Lauren (classic line).

**Visual effect**: Refined, conservative, effortlessly expensive. Every piece is simple but the quality is visible — the drape of cashmere, the hand of good wool. Palette is exclusively neutral — navy, cream, camel, charcoal, forest green, burgundy. No flash, no trend, no noise. Reads as generational wealth, tradition, and confidence.

**Pairs well with**:
- Neutral/earth palette — navy, cream, camel, charcoal
- Soft/natural lighting — gentle, not dramatic
- Classic settings — libraries, country estates, yachts, old cities
- Portra / film aesthetic — warm, refined tones match the clothing
- Quiet composition — nothing shouts
- Quality fabric texture — the cashmere, the wool, the cotton

**Conflicts with**:
- Bold logos or branding — old money whispers
- Bright/neon colors — too loud
- Streetwear energy — different cultural register
- Trendy/of-the-moment pieces — old money is timeless by definition

**Example combination**: Old money + cashmere + neutral palette + soft natural light + classic architecture → quietly expensive, fabric quality visible, no logos, understated elegance, inherited confidence

### Afrofuturist Fashion

**What it is**: Fashion blending African diaspora textiles, patterns, and ornamentation with futuristic/sci-fi elements — traditional beadwork alongside metallic fabrics, African prints in unconventional silhouettes, natural hair styled dramatically. See Art Movements for the broader Afrofuturism entry. Key references: Black Panther costumes, Janelle Monáe, Grace Jones.

**Pairs well with**: Metallic/chrome accents, bold color, cosmic backgrounds, dramatic lighting, sculptural hair.

---

### Cottagecore Fashion

**What it is**: The clothing component of the cottagecore aesthetic — linen dresses, floral prints, aprons, straw hats, handknit items, prairie dresses, soft romantic silhouettes. See Art Movements for the broader cottagecore entry.

**Pairs well with**: Golden hour, meadows, warm muted palette, Portra tones, soft diffused light, natural textures.

---

### Preppy / Ivy League

**What it is**: 1950s-60s American East Coast college culture. Ralph Lauren, Brooks Brothers, J.Crew. Sportswear-turned-casual from elite universities.

**Visual effect**: Clean, structured, affluent-but-approachable. Polo shirts, cable-knit sweaters (often draped over shoulders), chinos, Oxford shirts, loafers, plaid/tartan, navy blazers. Colors: navy, hunter green, burgundy, khaki, white. Everything fits well but never tight.

**Pairs well with**: Old money/quiet luxury, cottagecore (country estate), dark academia (scholarly), vintage 1950s-60s, soft natural light.

**Conflicts with**: Punk, streetwear, techwear, cyberpunk — anything rejecting institutional polish.

---

### Gothic / Goth Fashion

**What it is**: From 1970s-80s post-punk music scene (Bauhaus, Siouxsie Sioux, The Cure). Sub-branches: trad goth, romantic goth, Victorian goth, nu-goth, pastel goth, cybergoth.

**Visual effect**: Dominated by black. Lace, velvet, leather, fishnet, corsets, platform boots, silver jewelry (crosses, ankhs, bats), heavy dark eyeliner, pale skin, dark lips. Victorian goth adds high collars, cameos, parasols. Nu-goth adds minimalist edge.

**Pairs well with**: Avant-garde, Victorian vintage, dark academia, Lolita (gothic lolita crossover), cyberpunk (cybergoth), dramatic/low key lighting.

**Conflicts with**: Cottagecore, preppy, normcore, athleisure — anything bright, casual, or pastoral.

**Example combination**: Gothic fashion + flowing black velvet + silver jewelry + fog-shrouded cemetery + moonlight → romantic goth, dramatic and dark, pale skin against black, Victorian ornament

---

### Lolita (Japanese Fashion)

**What it is**: 1990s Harajuku subculture. Hyper-feminine, modesty-focused, inspired by Rococo and Victorian children's clothing. Sub-styles: Sweet (pastels, bows, cake motifs), Gothic (black, crosses), Classic (muted, Edwardian).

**Visual effect**: Bell-shaped skirts (always knee-length, petticoats), Peter Pan collars, lace, ribbons, bonnets, platform Mary Janes. Extremely structured silhouette. Fabric printed with motifs: roses, crosses, sweets, playing cards. "Living porcelain doll."

**Pairs well with**: Rococo (direct ancestor), cottagecore (Sweet Lolita), gothic (Gothic Lolita), anime styles, pastel palettes, soft lighting.

**Conflicts with**: Streetwear, techwear, grunge, military — anything rugged or deconstructed.

---

### Harajuku

**What it is**: Not a single style but a fashion movement from Tokyo's Harajuku district. Core principle: creative self-expression without rules. Encompasses Lolita, decora, fairy kei, visual kei, gyaru, and constant mutation.

**Visual effect**: Maximalist layering, clashing colors, mixing high and low, heavy accessorizing. Decora specifically: dozens of hair clips, plastic jewelry, layered colorful everything. Explosive, joyful, intentionally "too much."

**Pairs well with**: Pop art, anime, Y2K (gyaru), kawaii aesthetics, maximalism, saturated color.

**Conflicts with**: Minimalist, normcore, old money, dark academia — anything restrained or monochromatic.

---

### Normcore

**What it is**: Circa 2013-14 deliberate anti-fashion. Freedom through sameness. Uniqlo, Muji, Steve Jobs wardrobe logic.

**Visual effect**: Aggressively ordinary. Plain t-shirts, dad jeans, white sneakers, fleece, baseball caps. No logos. Neutral colors: grey, beige, white, navy, faded denim. Fits neither tight nor oversized — just normal. "Person who doesn't think about clothes" (but carefully chose this).

**Pairs well with**: Minimalist, documentary/street photography, urban settings, flat natural lighting.

**Conflicts with**: Haute couture, avant-garde, Harajuku, maximalism — anything attention-seeking.

---

### Dark Academia Fashion

**What it is**: Clothing component of the dark academia aesthetic. 1930s-40s British boarding school meets Dead Poets Society.

**Visual effect**: Tweed blazers, turtlenecks, corduroy, Oxford shoes, wool overcoats, pleated skirts, cable-knit vests, round glasses, leather satchels. Palette: brown, tan, cream, forest green, burgundy, charcoal. Textures: wool, tweed, leather, aged paper. Environments: libraries, autumn, old stone.

**Pairs well with**: Preppy (lighter sibling), gothic (darker sibling), vintage 1930s-40s, cottagecore (pastoral scholar), candlelight/warm tungsten, film grain.

**Conflicts with**: Y2K, cyberpunk, techwear, streetwear, Harajuku — anything modern, urban, or neon.

---

### Athleisure

**What it is**: Athletic wear as everyday fashion. 2010s rise of Lululemon, Nike lifestyle, Adidas Yeezy. Now a dominant contemporary silhouette.

**Visual effect**: Leggings, joggers, sports bras as tops, sneakers, windbreakers. Clean lines, body-conscious, stretchy technical fabrics. Colors range from monochrome black/grey to bold neons. Modern athleisure skews luxury: matching sets, muted earth tones, minimal branding.

**Pairs well with**: Minimalist, techwear (functional overlap), normcore, streetwear, gorpcore, clean bright lighting.

**Conflicts with**: Rococo, Victorian, Lolita, haute couture, dandyism — anything formal or historically costumed.

---

### Western / Cowboy

**What it is**: American frontier workwear romanticized through Hollywood, country music, rodeo. Periodically resurges in high fashion.

**Visual effect**: Cowboy boots, wide-brimmed hats, denim, leather (fringe, tooled belts), bolo ties, bandanas, plaid/flannel. Textures: worn leather, sun-faded denim, suede. Palette: dusty tan, brown, rust, denim blue, faded red, turquoise accents. Broad-shouldered, bootcut silhouettes.

**Pairs well with**: Vintage, bohemian (festival-western), Americana, golden hour desert lighting, warm film tones.

**Conflicts with**: Techwear, cyberpunk, Lolita, Harajuku — anything urban-futuristic or hyper-ornate.

**Example combination**: Western + fringed suede jacket + cowboy hat + desert highway + golden hour → frontier romance, warm leather and denim, turquoise accents, dusty Americana

---

### Military / Utilitarian

**What it is**: Fashion derived from military uniforms and functional workwear. Continuous influence from WWII surplus through punk to modern designers (Raf Simons, Helmut Lang).

**Visual effect**: Cargo pants, M-65 field jackets, bomber jackets (MA-1), combat boots, utility vests, epaulettes, camouflage. Palette: olive drab, khaki, navy, black, desert tan. Heavy on pockets, straps, hardware. Boxy, functional silhouettes.

**Pairs well with**: Techwear (functional overlap), punk, streetwear, gorpcore, grunge, minimalist (clean utilitarian).

**Conflicts with**: Lolita, rococo, cottagecore — anything delicate or ornamental.

---

### Dandyism / Dapper

**What it is**: The art of male self-presentation as supreme aesthetic achievement. From Beau Brummell's Regency era to modern Afro-dandyism.

**Visual effect**: Immaculately tailored three-piece suits, bold pocket squares, cravats, two-tone brogues, fedoras, waistcoats, watch chains. Fine wool, silk, velvet. Everything precise — sharp creases, perfect proportions. Colors range from conservative (charcoal, navy) to flamboyant (emerald, lavender, mustard).

**Pairs well with**: Vintage (Regency to 1960s), old money, dark academia, Regencycore, Art Deco settings, dramatic side lighting.

**Conflicts with**: Normcore, athleisure, grunge, streetwear, techwear — anything deliberately casual.

**Example combination**: Dandyism + emerald velvet three-piece suit + gold watch chain + Art Deco hotel lobby + warm dramatic light → impeccable tailoring, luxurious fabric, meticulous detail, Gatsby-era elegance

---

### Grunge Fashion

**What it is**: Seattle music scene (1989-1994) anti-fashion. Nirvana, Pearl Jam, Soundgarden. Thrift store aesthetics as cultural movement. Distinct from punk in apathy vs anger.

**Visual effect**: Oversized flannel shirts (often tied around waist), ripped jeans, band tees, Doc Martens, Converse, oversized cardigans, beanies, chokers. Intentionally sloppy layering. Unwashed, messy hair. Palette: washed-out plaid, faded black, olive, burgundy, dirty denim. Soft, worn, pilled fabrics.

**Pairs well with**: Punk (parent genre), vintage 1990s, bohemian, dark academia (scholarly grunge), disposable camera aesthetic.

**Conflicts with**: Preppy, dandyism, old money, Lolita — anything polished or precious.

---

### Rococo Fashion

**What it is**: French aristocratic fashion circa 1720-1789. Marie Antoinette era. The most ornate fashion period in Western history.

**Visual effect**: Extreme volume — panniers (wide hip structures), towering powdered wigs, corseted waists. Pastels: powder blue, pale pink, lavender, cream, gold. Silk brocade, lace, satin, taffeta. Ribbons, bows, ruffles, embroidery, pearls layered to excess. Men equally ornate: frock coats, breeches, jabots, powdered wigs, heeled shoes.

**Pairs well with**: Lolita (direct descendant), Regencycore (next era), cottagecore (French pastoral), pastel palettes, Baroque settings.

**Conflicts with**: Minimalist, normcore, techwear, military, grunge, punk — anything reductive or hard-edged.

---

### Regencycore / Bridgerton Aesthetic

**What it is**: Regency era (1795-1837) revived through Netflix's Bridgerton. Empire-waist silhouettes, Neoclassical elegance, English high society.

**Visual effect**: Empire-waist gowns (gathered below bust, flowing skirts), delicate muslin and cotton, puff sleeves, elbow-length gloves, bonnets, Spencer jackets. Colors: soft pastels, white, cream, periwinkle, sage, mauve. Pearls, cameos, tiaras. For men: tailcoats, cravats, Hessian boots. Everything has a vertical, column-like flow.

**Pairs well with**: Rococo (previous era), cottagecore, dandyism (Regency is its birthplace), old money, romantic art styles.

**Conflicts with**: Cyberpunk, techwear, streetwear, Y2K, punk — anything modern or aggressive.

---

### Cyberpunk Fashion

**What it is**: Distinct from techwear in narrative DNA. Techwear is functional futurism; cyberpunk is dystopian futurism. Blade Runner, Ghost in the Shell, The Matrix.

**Visual effect**: Neon-accented dark clothing, LED strips, holographic materials, transparent PVC panels, asymmetric cuts, respirator masks, chrome hardware, AR glasses. Predominantly black with electric blue, hot pink, neon green accents. Synthetic leather, latex, PVC, reflective/iridescent fabrics. More theatrical and narrative-driven than techwear.

**Pairs well with**: Techwear (functional cousin), anime, punk, streetwear, avant-garde, neon lighting, rain/wet surfaces.

**Conflicts with**: Cottagecore, Regencycore, rococo, preppy, old money — anything pastoral or traditionally elegant.

---

### Y2K Fashion

**What it is**: 1997-2004 millennium aesthetic. Britney, Destiny's Child, Paris Hilton, The Matrix. Massive revival since ~2020.

**Visual effect**: Low-rise everything, butterfly clips, tiny sunglasses, bedazzled rhinestone details, velour tracksuits, metallic fabrics, platform shoes, baby tees, chain belts, frosted lip gloss. Colors: hot pink, baby blue, silver/chrome, lavender, lime. Materials: PVC, faux fur, velour, metallic mesh. Two moods: bubbly pop (Paris Hilton) and dark digital (Matrix leather). Logo-heavy.

**Pairs well with**: Streetwear, cyberpunk (Matrix branch), Harajuku (pop branch), pop art, early CG aesthetics, flash photography.

**Conflicts with**: Dark academia, Regencycore, minimalist, old money — anything muted, scholarly, or historical.
