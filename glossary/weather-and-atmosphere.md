# Weather & Atmosphere

Environmental conditions that affect every other visual dimension — light quality, color temperature, mood, texture, and visibility. Weather terms are among the highest-leverage single-word prompt modifiers because they simultaneously change lighting, color, texture, and emotional register.

---

## Clear Conditions

### Clear Sky / Direct Sun

**What it is**: Cloudless sky, direct unobstructed sunlight. The quality and mood shifts dramatically by time of day (see Time of Day section below).

**Visual effect**: Hard, defined shadows. High contrast. Strong color saturation in direct light. Blue sky provides cool fill in shadows. The harshest conditions at midday, the most beautiful at golden hour.

**Pairs well with**: Hard midday sun (high contrast editorial), golden hour (warm romantic), landscape (clear visibility to horizon), architecture (strong shadow patterns).

**Conflicts with**: Soft/diffused light (clear sky IS hard light at midday), moody/atmospheric (too much visibility), overcast-dependent aesthetics.

---

### Overcast / Cloudy

**What it is**: Cloud cover acting as a giant diffuser. The sky becomes one enormous soft light source.

**Visual effect**: Soft, even, shadowless or near-shadowless illumination. Slightly cool color temperature (~6500K+). Colors appear saturated but without contrast — vivid but flat. Skin looks smooth and even. Reads as contemplative, muted, melancholic, or quietly beautiful.

**Pairs well with**: Portrait photography (flattering, even), product photography outdoors (accurate color), melancholic mood, muted/desaturated palette, green landscapes (overcast makes foliage vivid), street photography.

**Conflicts with**: Dramatic shadow play, warm/golden mood, high-contrast compositions, sun-dependent lighting setups.

---

## Precipitation

### Rain

**What it is**: The single most transformative weather condition for visual work. Rain changes every surface in the frame — asphalt becomes a mirror, skin glistens, glass distorts, neon signs double in puddles.

**Visual effect**: **Wet surfaces become reflective** — this is rain's primary visual gift. Every surface gains specular quality. Puddles create mirror reflections of light sources. Pavement doubles neon signs, streetlights, and headlights. The air itself becomes visible (falling rain catches light). Clothing clings. Hair becomes textured. Overcast sky provides soft ambient, but artificial lights punch through dramatically.

**Pairs well with**:
- Neon/artificial lighting — reflections in wet surfaces multiply every light source (the Blade Runner effect)
- Cyberpunk — rain is the genre's native weather
- Film noir — rain-slicked streets are the signature
- Wong Kar-wai — wet glass, neon reflections, romantic melancholy
- Night scenes — rain + darkness + artificial light = maximum atmosphere
- CineStill 800T — halation on rain-reflected lights
- Direct flash — flash catching individual raindrops mid-fall

**Conflicts with**: Dry/arid aesthetics, bright/airy mood, clear sharp backgrounds (rain adds haze), cottagecore (unless gentle spring rain).

**Example combination**: Night rain + neon signs + wet street reflections + anamorphic lens flares + shallow DOF → every light source doubled in puddles, blue and pink streaks from neon, rain catching backlight, cinematic atmosphere

---

### Snow

**What it is**: Falling and accumulated snow. White ground cover acts as a giant reflector bouncing light upward (under-lighting). Falling snowflakes catch light.

**Visual effect**: **High-key natural environment** — the white ground raises the ambient light level. Fill light from below (reflected off snow) softens shadows under chin and brow. Falling snow adds particle texture to the air. Color palette shifts cool — blue shadows on white snow. Accumulated snow simplifies the landscape (hides detail, creates clean white shapes). Reads as quiet, clean, isolated, magical, or cold depending on context.

**Pairs well with**: Cool/blue color grade, high-key natural, quiet/contemplative mood, isolation (tracks in fresh snow), holiday/winter warmth (warm interior light against cold exterior), shallow DOF (snowflakes become bokeh), backlit falling snow (each flake glows).

**Conflicts with**: Warm/tropical, saturated warm color, green/lush landscapes, summer aesthetics.

**Example combination**: Falling snow + backlit by streetlight + warm tungsten window glow + figure in dark coat + shallow DOF → snowflakes glowing as bokeh in foreground, warm/cool contrast between interior and exterior, quiet and magical

---

### Mist / Light Rain / Drizzle

**What it is**: Fine moisture in the air — between fog and rain. Surfaces are damp but not flowing. The air has a slight veil.

**Visual effect**: Subtle atmospheric softening. Surfaces are damp (slight sheen) but not mirror-wet like heavy rain. Air has a faint haze. Colors are slightly muted. Reads as gentle, dreamy, British/Pacific Northwest, literary.

**Pairs well with**: Overcast sky, muted palette, green landscapes (lush and wet), literary mood, Saul Leiter (shooting through wet glass), cottagecore (gentle spring rain).

---

## Atmospheric Effects

### Fog / Heavy Mist

**What it is**: Dense moisture in the air reducing visibility. Objects fade with distance. The air itself becomes a visible medium.

**Visual effect**: **Atmospheric perspective at extreme** — objects lose contrast and detail with distance, fading to gray/white. Near objects are sharp, mid-ground softens, far objects are silhouettes or invisible. Depth layers become obvious. Light sources get halos and bloom. Reads as mysterious, isolated, eerie, dreamlike, or cinematic.

**Pairs well with**:
- Rim light / backlight — light catches fog from behind, creating volumetric god rays
- Horror / mystery — obscured visibility creates tension
- Silhouettes — figures emerge from or disappear into fog
- Film noir — fog + streetlight = atmospheric crime scene
- Long focal length — telephoto compression through fog layers
- Landscape — depth layers revealed by fog filling valleys

**Conflicts with**: Full visibility (fog removes it), crisp detail at distance, bright sunny conditions (fog burns off in sun).

**Example combination**: Dense fog + single streetlight + figure approaching + rim-lit from behind → silhouette emerging from white void, volumetric light rays, depth completely obscured, noir mystery

---

### Haze / Smog

**What it is**: Lighter than fog — a subtle atmospheric veil that reduces contrast at distance without obscuring visibility. Urban smog, summer heat haze, wildfire smoke.

**Visual effect**: Slightly reduced contrast globally. Warm-tinted (smog) or neutral (haze). Distant objects soften but remain visible. The atmosphere has weight. Reads as hot, urban, polluted, or dreamy depending on context.

**Pairs well with**: Golden hour (haze amplifies warm color), urban landscapes (city haze is a real phenomenon), telephoto compression (amplified by atmospheric layers), vintage/retro (older photos had more atmospheric haze), Apocalypse Now aesthetic (jungle haze + fire).

**Conflicts with**: Crisp clear air, high-contrast photography, cool/blue palette (haze is warm).

---

### Dust / Sand

**What it is**: Airborne particulates — dust storms, desert wind, construction sites, dirt roads. Visible particles in the air.

**Visual effect**: Warm amber/golden atmospheric veil. Hard backlighting catches individual particles — the air sparkles. Surfaces accumulate dust (matte quality). Reduced visibility in heavy dust. Reads as hot, rugged, frontier, apocalyptic, or earthy.

**Pairs well with**: Western/desert aesthetics, golden hour (dust + warm light = golden atmosphere), practical lighting (headlights through dust), Mad Max/post-apocalyptic, military, construction/industrial, hard backlight (every particle catches light).

**Conflicts with**: Clean/pristine, clinical, urban (unless construction context), cool palettes.

---

### Smoke

**What it is**: Visible combustion product in the air — from fires, cigarettes, machines, fog machines. Thicker and more defined than haze, catches light dramatically.

**Visual effect**: **Volumetric light becomes visible** — any beam of light passing through smoke creates a visible shaft (god rays). Smoke drifts and curls, creating organic movement. Warm-tinted if from fire, neutral if theatrical. Reads as dramatic, cinematic, dangerous, atmospheric.

**Pairs well with**:
- Hard directional light — creates visible beams through smoke
- Fire/ember particles — smoke + fire is natural
- Film noir — cigarette smoke in a beam of light
- Concert/stage lighting — theatrical haze
- Chiaroscuro — smoke makes the light sculptural
- Blade Runner — smoke + neon + rain = the trinity

**Conflicts with**: Clean/clinical, bright/airy, outdoor/natural (unless campfire or wildfire).

---

### Steam / Vapor

**What it is**: Water vapor — from hot food, hot surfaces in rain, bathroom, industrial vents, manholes in cold cities.

**Visual effect**: Soft, warm, rising. Lighter and more translucent than smoke. Catches backlight beautifully. Rising steam from hot food signals freshness and temperature. Street steam (NYC manholes) adds urban atmosphere. Reads as warm, fresh, urban, or industrial.

**Pairs well with**: Food photography (steam = hot and fresh), hot beverage (rising steam from coffee), urban night (manhole steam + streetlight), backlight (steam glows), bathroom/sauna (intimate, warm).

## Time of Day

The single most impactful prompt dimension after subject. Each time period defines light direction, color temperature, shadow angle, and emotional register.

### Dawn / First Light

**Visual**: Deep blue sky transitioning to warm horizon. Extremely soft, directional. Cool overall with warm edge where sun is about to rise. Long shadows. Quiet, fresh, untouched. Almost no one is awake.

**Color temperature**: ~4000-5000K, shifting warm as sun nears horizon.

**Pairs with**: Landscape, solitude, new beginnings, running/exercise, mist on water, birds.

---

### Early Morning (7-9am)

**Visual**: Low warm sun, long golden shadows, clean air (no midday haze). Similar to golden hour but the world is waking up, not winding down — the mood is fresh and forward-looking rather than nostalgic.

**Pairs with**: Lifestyle photography, jogging/health, coffee, commute, dewy surfaces, fresh light.

---

### Midday (11am-1pm)

**Visual**: Sun directly overhead. Short, hard shadows directly below subjects. Harsh, unflattering on faces (deep eye socket shadows). Maximum contrast. Maximum color saturation in direct sun. Blue fill in shadows from open sky. The "worst" light for conventional portraits but powerful for graphic/editorial work.

**Pairs with**: Hard editorial fashion, graphic shadow patterns, saturated color, harsh texture reveal, street photography (high contrast urban), documentary (unflinching).

**Conflicts with**: Flattering portraiture, soft/romantic, golden hour warmth.

---

### Afternoon (2-4pm)

**Visual**: Sun lowering but still relatively high. Warm light from the side. Shadows lengthening. The transition period — warmer than midday but not yet golden hour. Common for event photography, outdoor weddings, lifestyle.

**Pairs with**: Lifestyle, outdoor events, warm natural, environmental portraits.

---

### Golden Hour (last hour before sunset)

**Visual**: See lighting.md for full entry. Warm (~3500K), soft, directional, long shadows, gentle contrast, everything glows. The most universally flattering natural light.

---

### Sunset

**Visual**: The dramatic moment. Vivid orange/red/purple sky. Sun at horizon — subjects can be backlit (silhouette) or front-lit (last warm light on face). The sky becomes the subject. Maximum color in the atmosphere.

**Pairs with**: Silhouettes, vivid sky, romantic, dramatic, wide angle (maximize sky), epic landscape.

---

### Blue Hour (20-40 min after sunset)

**Visual**: See lighting.md for full entry. Deep saturated blue sky. Artificial lights (warm) against blue sky (cool) — natural complementary contrast. The color of twilight.

---

### Night

**Visual**: No natural ambient. All light is artificial — streetlights, neon, headlights, windows, practicals. High contrast between lit and unlit areas. The darkness itself is a compositional element. Color is determined entirely by the artificial sources.

**Pairs with**: Neon, cyberpunk, film noir, CineStill 800T, long exposure (light trails), urban, mystery, horror, nightlife.

**Conflicts with**: Natural light, golden hour, high-key, bright/airy.

---

### Deep Night / 3am

**Visual**: Minimal artificial light. Near-total darkness with isolated pools of illumination — a single streetlight, a vending machine, a phone screen. The emptiness is the content. Reads as liminal, lonely, insomniac, contemplative, eerie.

**Pairs with**: Liminal space aesthetic, noir, horror, isolation, long exposure, available light only, Crewdson suburban uncanny.

## Mood → Visual Mapping

Reverse-engineering: start from the emotion, derive the visual recipe.

### Melancholic
**Visual recipe**: Desaturated palette, cool blue/gray tones, overcast or blue hour light, negative space, isolated subject, shallow DOF (subject sharp, world blurred), slight film grain, muted color, long shadows or flat light.

### Energetic
**Visual recipe**: High saturation, warm or vivid complementary colors, diagonal composition, tight crop, direct flash or hard light, motion blur or speed lines, low angle (power), wide angle (distortion/dynamism).

### Intimate
**Visual recipe**: Shallow DOF (f/1.4-f/2), warm color (tungsten/candlelight), close framing (MCU or CU), soft directional light (window), visible grain, practical lighting, quiet negative space around the subject.

### Ominous / Dread
**Visual recipe**: Low-key lighting, under-lighting or top-lighting (unnatural shadow direction), cool desaturated with one warm accent, fog or haze, wide angle for distortion, low angle looking up, crushed blacks, liminal space, deep night.

### Nostalgic
**Visual recipe**: Warm vintage grade (lifted blacks, amber tint), film grain (Portra 400 or Kodak Gold), soft overexposed highlights, golden hour, slightly faded saturation, vignetting, medium format (rich tonality), subjects looking away from camera.

### Serene / Peaceful
**Visual recipe**: Soft diffused light (overcast or open shade), analogous cool or warm palette, wide shot with negative space, deep DOF (everything sharp and equal), horizontal composition, symmetry or balanced framing, gentle natural environment.

### Chaotic
**Visual recipe**: Dutch angle, multiple competing light sources (mixed color temperature), tight crop with elements exiting frame, high saturation clashing colors, motion blur, grain, direct flash, wide angle distortion, busy composition, no negative space.

### Luxurious
**Visual recipe**: Medium format or large format (rich tonality), soft directional light with specular highlights, warm gold/amber, shallow DOF, glossy/reflective surfaces, dark background, negative space, precise composition, beauty dish or clamshell lighting.

### Eerie / Uncanny
**Visual recipe**: Slightly wrong lighting (motivated from an unusual source), desaturated with one oversaturated element, liminal space, deep focus (everything sharp — nothing escapes attention), slight dutch angle, overcast or fluorescent, subjects slightly off-center, empty architecture.

### Joyful
**Visual recipe**: High-key, bright warm light (golden hour or overcast + warm grade), high saturation, wide smile/expression, wide angle for expansive feeling, vivid color accents, motion/action, slightly overexposed, candid/unposed framing.
